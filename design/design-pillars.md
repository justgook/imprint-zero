> **Accepted** — These ordered pillars protect the game while implementation details change. A representative encounter must still prove that the current design fulfills them.

| Priority | Pillar | Requires | Rejects |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Action mastery comes first | Readable threats, responsive control, learnable execution, and satisfying improvement without progression rewards | Narrative, spectacle, or statistics compensating for weak action |
| 2 | Character choice transforms play | Distinct combat decisions, Character-owned optional-route verbs, and Specializations that vary rather than replace Character identity | Cosmetic Characters, minor statistical variation, or critical paths invalidated by selection |
| 3 | Discovery changes understanding and progression | Lore or memory paired with a Specialization, equipment access, Overdrive, or hidden campaign evidence | Passive lore collectibles, context-free keys, and explicit secret-ending checklists |

## Action mastery comes first

The player should improve by reading threats, executing movement and combat, understanding a Character's strengths, and applying route knowledge. Difficulty grows through patterns, spatial demands, enemy combinations, and decisions—not only larger health and damage values.

## Character choice transforms play

After the controlled introduction, every Mission critical path supports every available Character and Specialization. Character-owned verbs gate only optional routes, shortcuts, Imprints, equipment opportunities, and other rewards. Equipment may tune a Specialization but cannot grant another Character's signature verb.

## Discovery changes understanding and progression

> **TODO — Complete Imprint example:** Document one Imprint from Character-specific memory scene through its progression result and any later reinterpretation.

Every Imprint adds lore through a memory or cutscene and produces an explicit progression result. Specialization Imprints unlock Specializations; Equipment Imprints expose acquisition opportunities; Overdrive Imprints reveal the concealed system; biome Memory Imprints provide hidden campaign evidence.

Discovery should encourage inference. Character gates reveal whom to bring back without revealing their contents, and biome Memory Imprints initially appear to be optional lore rather than a four-part true-route checklist.

## Supporting presentation principle

**Familiarity without repetition** makes the action language and broad Character archetypes immediately legible while preserving an original cast, world, challenge set, and journey. It supports the pillars but cannot substitute for them.

## Validation rule

A pillar remains useful only while it has:

1. a concise statement;
2. observable consequences during play;
3. at least one feature it requires;
4. at least one tempting feature it rejects;
5. a practical playtest or example that could disprove it.
