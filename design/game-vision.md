## Purpose

This page defines what game is being made, why it should exist, and what must remain true while campaign details change. It owns the core fantasy, differentiation, intended audience, and creative boundaries. Narrative truth, campaign structure, platform scope, and current validation work belong on their linked canonical pages.

## Title and tagline

> **Accepted** — **Imprint Zero** — *You know how to fight. Not who you are.*

The name is treated as the game's title unless a later availability, legal, or positioning review creates a concrete reason to revisit it.

## Core game fantasy

> **Accepted** — Deploy as one of four distinct combat specialists into handcrafted side-scrolling Missions. Master how that character moves and fights while recovering evidence that changes both capability and understanding of who the crew might be.

Action mastery is the primary fantasy. Running, jumping, shooting, route reading, and character-specific execution must remain satisfying without narrative context. Identity discovery differentiates that action rather than compensating for it: Memory Imprints expose uncertain evidence while unlocking Specializations, equipment access, Overdrive, or hidden campaign routes.

The crew's exact origin and OPERATOR's identity remain deliberately uncertain. [[Current Direction|Current direction]] owns the latest narrative boundaries; [[Missions/Overview|Campaign progression]] owns campaign structure.

## Player promise

> **Accepted** — Master the same handcrafted campaign through fundamentally different crew members, then revisit visible mysteries with the Character capable of reaching them.

Character selection changes combat decisions, traversal opportunities, optional routes, and access to discoveries while preserving a completable critical path. Identity evidence gives those discoveries narrative consequence without resolving the crew into one certain origin. The Standard ending must provide a satisfying standalone completion; deeper routes reward players who infer and pursue missing evidence. [[Gameplay/Progression|Progression]] owns route eligibility, completion reporting, and cross-campaign persistence.

**Familiarity without repetition** supports this promise as a presentation principle. Players should quickly understand the action language and crew archetypes, but encounter an original cast, world, challenge set, and journey.

## Differentiation

> **Accepted** — Character selection is the primary way to experience the authored campaign differently. Each crew member establishes a distinct movement and combat identity; their Specializations provide deliberate variations within that identity.

Every Memory Imprint adds lore through a memory or cutscene while unlocking progression: a Specialization, access to acquiring equipment, the concealed Overdrive system, or hidden campaign evidence. Shared Imprints preserve common evidence while foregrounding a memory fragment, interpretation, or reaction specific to the deployed Character. Equipment may tune reach, timing, resource use, damage profile, or tactical emphasis within an established Character and Specialization play style. It must not grant another character's signature verb, erase the selected identity, or turn one crew member into another.

Retro presentation is not sufficient differentiation. The game must be recognizable through its original crew, materially different approaches to the same handcrafted challenges, actionable identity discoveries, and industrial cyberpunk journey.

## Mission compatibility

| Campaign phase | Character rule |
|---|---|
| Controlled introduction | Explicit onboarding exceptions progressively introduce the crew and may force or switch Characters. |
| Open campaign | Every Mission's critical path must be completable by every available Character and Specialization. |
| Optional routes | Character-owned signature verbs may reveal shortcuts, alternate spaces, Imprints, equipment access, and other rewards. Every Specialization of that Character retains the route-access verb. |

After the controlled introduction, Character gating changes exploration and tactical opportunity—not critical-path eligibility. [[Gameplay/Progression|Progression]] owns the exact transition and revisit window.

## Intended audience

> **Accepted** — The game is for players who enjoy precise, readable 2D action; mastering fundamentally different Characters; revisiting handcrafted Missions after noticing inaccessible routes; and piecing together an ambiguous science-fiction mystery without an explicit completion checklist.

Replay rewards curiosity, but the Standard ending must satisfy players who stop after one completion. Nostalgia may attract attention, but the game must remain worthwhile without attachment to a specific reference. It does not primarily serve expectations of procedural runs, constant randomized rewards, continuous bullet hell, or hardware-authentic inconvenience.

## Creative boundaries

> **Accepted** — These boundaries protect the vision while implementation details change.

- Action must remain satisfying before narrative context or progression rewards are considered.
- Character selection must change play; Specializations vary a Character, and equipment must not cross signature-verb boundaries.
- From HUB1 onward, no valid Character or Specialization may be unable to finish a Mission's critical path.
- Every Imprint must pair lore or a memory scene with an explicit progression result.
- No ending may resolve the crew's origin or OPERATOR's identity beyond doubt.
- Hidden-route discovery must feel inferred by the player: do not expose biome Memory Imprints as a route checklist or announce the true ending in advance.
- Performance statistics may suggest completeness but must never gate an ending or hidden route.
- The Standard ending must stand as a satisfying completion; deeper routes add interpretation rather than repairing an intentionally incomplete first ending.
- Create an original cast, world, challenges, and assets; evoke remembered conventions without reproducing a particular game.
- Do not use nostalgia or speculative content volume as a substitute for a strong representative encounter.
