## Perceived identity

> **Accepted** — **OPERATOR** is the institutional designation used for the consistent voice coordinating the crew's Missions.

The crew experiences OPERATOR as one recognizable operational entity. This does not establish whether OPERATOR is:

- one human being;
- an artificial intelligence;
- a constructed voice or persona;
- a role occupied by multiple people;
- an interface for an institution;
- or an intermediary relaying orders from an unknown authority.

“Operator” describes the relationship the crew can observe: the voice interprets objectives, coordinates deployments, and responds to operational events. It does not prove who authors the orders.

## Campaign function

OPERATOR:

- delivers or clarifies Mission objectives;
- frames each deployment's immediate public benefit;
- acknowledges completion and extraction;
- interprets institutional records for the crew;
- controls or communicates access to deployment information;
- discourages investigation when evidence conflicts with the accepted operation;
- may relay information from a command structure the crew cannot perceive directly.

> **Open question** — Which actions OPERATOR performs personally, which are automated, and which are merely communicated on behalf of another authority?

## Presentation and voice

> **Accepted** — OPERATOR is introduced as a voice during active play, not through a body reveal, biography, portrait, or opening cinematic.

The voice should be:

- calm and concise;
- operational rather than theatrical;
- reassuring enough that obedience initially feels normal;
- capable of dismissing contradictions without sounding openly malicious;
- recognizably consistent across Missions;
- ambiguous in gender, age, embodiment, and physical location unless later evidence resolves one of them.

The late-20th-century mission-control tone should create familiarity without directly imitating a specific existing character.

## Introduction role

During [[Missions/M01|Cold Deployment]], OPERATOR provides the objective while [[Characters/Rook|ROOK]] is already moving. The operation and radio coordination feel routine to the crew.

> **Accepted** — An ordinary terminal interface briefly displays ROOK under another designation marked killed in action. The clue receives no camera emphasis, pause, or spoken reaction from OPERATOR or the crew. The interface continues its normal completion flow, leaving it uncertain whether OPERATOR noticed or influenced the transition.

The presentation must:

1. remain easily overlooked on a first playthrough;
2. avoid telling the player that the record is important;
3. preserve uncertainty about OPERATOR's knowledge and control;
4. become recognizable in retrospect or on another campaign;
5. avoid interrupting the action with explanation.

## Relationship with the crew

The crew begins by treating OPERATOR as familiar operational support. Conditioning makes compliance feel safer and more natural than questioning the source or authority of an order.

- **ROOK:** initially treats OPERATOR's framing as routine.
- **VECTOR:** sounds operationally familiar during the introduction; the first explicit expression of doubt remains to be placed later.
- **RAM:** relationship requires clarification after RAM joins independently during a boss Encounter.
- **RELAY:** may recognize infrastructure or terminology that OPERATOR presents as unfamiliar legacy systems.

The player's rebellion begins with recognizing that OPERATOR's explanation can be questioned, not with immediate certainty that OPERATOR is the ultimate antagonist.

## Demonstrated knowledge

The introduction does not prove that OPERATOR notices, understands, or suppresses the killed-in-action record. Later evidence must establish knowledge or control through observable behavior rather than inference from the terminal transition.

Later Mission pages must distinguish:

- information OPERATOR states;
- information OPERATOR could reasonably infer;
- access OPERATOR visibly exercises;
- and knowledge attributed to OPERATOR only by crew suspicion.

## Relationship to the concealed project

> **Accepted** — Early Missions genuinely address local needs while restoring infrastructure, recovering material, or granting access later reused by an identity-control project. Later Missions expose consequences the crew did not expect without retroactively making the original benefit false. OPERATOR coordinates those Missions, but the crew does not know whether OPERATOR designed the project, knowingly serves it, partially understands it, or merely relays its recovery orders.

The institution or authority behind the project must not be collapsed into OPERATOR until evidence establishes that relationship.

## Identity question

Is OPERATOR guiding the crew through someone else's orders, protecting them from information they are conditioned not to process, administering the identity project directly, or functioning as another manufactured identity inside the same system?
