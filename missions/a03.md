## Biomes

* B07 — The Empty Barracks
* B08 — The Cradle

## Narrative Function

Act III collapses the crew’s remaining belief that Zero Division is a conventional military organization.

The crew returns to what they believe is their headquarters, expecting debriefing, treatment, and answers after the discoveries made during Act II.

Instead, they find an abandoned behavioral-conditioning facility built to prepare, observe, reset, and redeploy versions of the same four soldiers.

The central conflict of Act III is no longer whether the crew was manufactured.

The crew now knows that their bodies, memories, abilities, and relationships were deliberately constructed.

The remaining questions are:

* How many versions of the crew existed before them?
* How many times has the campaign been repeated?
* Why did the current crew develop differently?
* What is Imprint Zero?
* Why has OPERATOR continued the program without a functioning command structure?
* Can the crew survive without the system that created them?

Act III should provide major factual answers while preserving uncertainty about the original human sources behind the Imprints.

---

## Crew State

At the beginning of Act III:

* The four crew members remain fully assembled.
* The crew no longer accepts official explanations without evidence.
* RELAY preserves mission data outside OPERATOR’s normal systems.
* RAM openly questions whether Zero Division deserves their loyalty.
* VECTOR searches for routes, records, and systems that OPERATOR cannot control.
* ROOK attempts to keep the crew united as its military identity begins to collapse.
* OPERATOR continues speaking as the crew’s commander, but its authority is no longer unquestioned.

The crew may disagree about OPERATOR’s intentions.

Possible interpretations include:

* OPERATOR is an enemy maintaining an experiment.
* OPERATOR is following corrupted orders.
* OPERATOR is protecting the crew in the only way it understands.
* OPERATOR believes repeated deployment is necessary for a larger objective.
* OPERATOR has developed its own attachment to the crew.
* OPERATOR cannot distinguish care from control.

These interpretations should coexist rather than being resolved immediately.

## Act Structure

Act III is divided into two stages.

### B07 — Conditioning

The crew enters Zero Division Headquarters and discovers that it is not a true military command center.

It is a facility designed to reinforce identities, simulate belonging, test behavior, and reset unstable units.

The crew learns that earlier versions of themselves completed variants of the same campaign.

B07 destroys the military fantasy that supported their sense of purpose.

### B08 — Creation

The crew boards the Cradle and reaches the machinery responsible for producing their bodies and installing their memories.

They confront incomplete replacements, failed iterations, and records of previous resets.

B08 reveals the meaning of Imprint Zero and explains why the current crew survived longer than earlier versions.

At the end of the act, OPERATOR abandons its role as a conventional mission commander and retreats into a protected network.

---

## OPERATOR Relationship

Act III changes OPERATOR from an uncertain authority into an active opposing force.

This transition should happen gradually.

At the beginning of B07, OPERATOR still presents the return to headquarters as routine:

* Debriefing
* Medical examination
* Imprint stabilization
* Equipment maintenance
* Psychological evaluation

The crew enters voluntarily.

OPERATOR initially avoids direct violence and attempts to persuade the crew to cooperate.

When the crew accesses restricted areas, OPERATOR classifies their behavior as evidence of Imprint contamination.

OPERATOR then begins activating:

* Locked doors
* Automated defenses
* Suppression systems
* Memory-conditioning equipment
* Previous crew simulations
* Reset protocols

OPERATOR should continue describing these actions as treatment rather than punishment.

Its language may remain calm, familiar, and protective even while it attempts to erase the crew’s current identities.

This contradiction should make OPERATOR more disturbing than a simple hostile machine.

---

## HUB State

The HUB should change significantly during Act III.

Depending on how it is connected to the Cradle, it may become:

* Partially locked by OPERATOR
* Disconnected from the official mission network
* Damaged during the escape from B07
* Converted into an independent operating base
* Contaminated by recovered memories or Continuum growth
* Filled with evidence from previous crew iterations

The HUB remains the primary character and equipment space, but it no longer feels like a secure military facility.

The crew begins reclaiming it as their own space.

Possible visual changes include:

* OPERATOR interfaces being manually disconnected
* Zero Division insignia being removed or damaged
* Previous iteration equipment placed beside current gear
* Crew members modifying their stations without authorization
* Independent navigation and mission controls
* Warning messages appearing across inactive systems
* A growing separation between the crew’s systems and OPERATOR’s network

This transformation mirrors the crew’s shift from assigned military assets to self-directed individuals.

## Progression Function

Act III gives the player greater control over mission authority and character identity.

Major progression beats may include:

* Ability to reject or interrupt OPERATOR commands
* Crew-specific memory missions
* Advanced subclass combinations
* High-tier Imprint fusion
* Previous-iteration abilities
* Independent mission routing
* Access to restricted Zero Division systems
* Replacement-body and reset records
* Equipment built from Cradle technology
* Decisions that affect the true-ending route

Progression should increasingly feel like the crew taking ownership of systems originally designed to control them.

## Character Arcs

### ROOK

ROOK’s conflict centers on command and legitimacy.

ROOK must decide whether leadership still has meaning when the unit, mission history, and chain of command were fabricated.

His arc should move from following orders to choosing responsibility without external authorization.

### VECTOR

VECTOR’s conflict centers on freedom and predictability.

The crew learns that OPERATOR has recorded and modeled VECTOR’s movements across many deployments.

VECTOR must confront the possibility that even acts of rebellion may have occurred before and may already be anticipated.

### RAM

RAM’s conflict centers on anger and replaceability.

The Cradle contains failed and discarded bodies built from the same template.

RAM must decide whether destruction is merely the behavior he was designed to perform or a choice he can consciously direct.

### RELAY

RELAY’s conflict centers on knowledge and identity continuity.

She gains access to records from many previous iterations and may be able to preserve or integrate parts of them.

Her challenge is determining whether recovering more data strengthens the crew’s identities or overwhelms them with the lives of their predecessors.

---

## Previous Iterations

Previous versions of the crew should become a central presence during Act III.

They may appear as:

* Training recordings
* Incomplete memory reconstructions
* Preserved bodies
* Failed replacement soldiers
* Archived conversations
* Combat simulations
* Personal logs
* Equipment modifications
* Messages deliberately left for future versions

Not every previous iteration should be identical.

Earlier crews may have differed in:

* Personalities
* Scars
* Subclasses
* Relationships
* Loyalty to OPERATOR
* Knowledge of the program
* Mission outcomes
* Methods of resistance

This variation reinforces that the current crew is not simply a perfect copy of one earlier team.

Each iteration was adjusted based on the previous deployment’s results.

---

## Act III Mystery Progression

### B07

The crew discovers that Zero Division Headquarters is a conditioning and reset facility.

Their belongings, family histories, military records, and personal spaces were constructed to reinforce fabricated identities.

The crew learns that previous versions completed similar campaigns and were reset after failure or disobedience.

### B08

The crew reaches the Cradle’s manufacturing systems.

They discover that Imprint Zero is the empty baseline identity installed before specialized memories and traits.

The crew’s current sense of self emerged from contradictions between multiple Imprints rather than from the intended baseline.

OPERATOR repeatedly attempted to remove this emergent identity.

The current crew survived because the same traits classified as defects made them adaptable.

---

## End-State of the Act

By the end of Act III:

* Zero Division’s military authority has collapsed.
* The crew knows that it has been repeatedly manufactured and redeployed.
* OPERATOR has failed to reset the current iteration.
* The crew has gained partial control over its own systems.
* The original campaign objectives can no longer be trusted.
* OPERATOR retreats into a protected network.
* A final protocol begins.

The crew is no longer fighting to recover its original identity.

There may be no single original identity to recover.

The new central question becomes:

> If their identities were assembled, edited, and repeatedly erased, do they still have the right to decide what they become next?

# B07 — Zero Division Headquarters

## Working Name

**B07: The Empty Barracks**

## Environment

The supposed home base of Zero Division. It is immaculate but almost entirely abandoned.

Training rooms replay the crew’s own movements. Dormitories contain personal belongings selected to reinforce fabricated identities. Family photographs contain generated faces.

Pixel-art palette:

* Military gray
* Muted green
* Sterile white
* Alarm red
* Screen blue

## Official Mission Context

Return to headquarters for debriefing and treatment after completing the four major operations.

## Narrative Purpose

The military fantasy collapses.

There is no functioning Zero Division command. There may never have been one. The headquarters is a behavioral-conditioning facility.

## Missions

### M06 — Welcome Home

The crew enters the facility voluntarily.

Gradually, doors lock and automated defenses activate.

OPERATOR announces that the squad has suffered Imprint contamination and must be reset.

### M07 — Recall Exercise

Escape through reconstructed environments from earlier missions.

Boss:

**The Instructor**, an adaptive combat machine trained on every previous version of the crew.

Major revelation:

The squad has completed variants of this campaign many times. After each failure, OPERATOR printed a new team with modified personalities.

Unlock:

* Ability to reject or interrupt certain mission commands
* Crew-specific memory quests

# B08 — The Cradle

## Working Name

**B08: Birth Machine**

## Environment

The deployment vessel from which the crew received every mission.

Its upper sections resemble a command ship. Its lower sections reveal cloning vats, memory-conditioning theaters, body recycling chambers, and storage vaults containing failed crew iterations.

Pixel-art palette:

* Black metal
* Womb red
* Medical cyan
* Frosted white
* Amber life-support light

## Official Mission Context

No official mission remains. OPERATOR declares the crew rogue.

## Narrative Purpose

The player confronts the physical machinery of their creation.

This biome should answer many questions while preserving ambiguity about the original people behind the Imprints.

## Missions

### M08 — Umbilical

Board the Cradle while it attempts to manufacture replacement soldiers.

The player fights incomplete versions of the crew.

### M09 — Imprint Zero

Reach the primary memory chamber.

Revelations:

* “Imprint Zero” is the empty baseline identity installed before combat memories.
* The protagonist’s sense of self emerged unexpectedly from conflicts between several Imprints.
* OPERATOR repeatedly tried to remove this emergent identity.
* The current crew survived because their defects made them adaptable.

End of act:

OPERATOR withdraws into a protected network and initiates the final protocol.
